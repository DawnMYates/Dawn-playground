## Market-cycle assessment

Classification: Late-cycle speculative expansion, with a Distribution-watch overlay.

Why this label: Prices remain near cycle highs — closer this Friday than last Friday — while margin debt is historically large and still growing on a year-over-year basis, valuations (CAPE and Buffett) are in extreme long-horizon territory, and leadership is narrow. That is the signature of late-cycle speculative expansion. The overlay is distribution watch, not confirmed distribution: internals are still weak relative to cap-weight, small caps lagged this week, and July showed that leverage can reverse quickly. Confirmed deleveraging is not the right label while the last FINRA print rose and indexes reclaimed the mid-7700s.

What would change the classification:

- To confirmed deleveraging: a second consecutive FINRA monthly decline in margin debt coinciding with a break in major indexes, widening HY OAS through 4–5%, and a VIX regime that stays above ~20 with term-structure backwardation.
- To distribution watch as the primary label: failed retests of the August 13 high, persistent negative breadth after this week’s bounce, and equity-financing/SOFR stress that stops looking technical.
- Back toward markup / expansion: broadening breadth that includes the Russell 2000 and equal-weight, a cooling in YoY margin-debt growth without a price crash, and less crowded mega-cap/AI leadership.

Concise interpretation: The market looks more resilient on the surface this Friday than last Friday and still fragile underneath. It is not actively deteriorating — credit is quiet at the index level, VIX closed calm in contango, and the S&P 500 used the week to move back toward its high. The pressure gauges that matter for cycle risk — margin leverage, concentration, and valuation — remain elevated. These are pressure gauges, not precise market-timing clocks.
